Long Acting Octreotide for Prevention of Variceal Bleeding

Author

Sammie Rosen and Keith Hazleton

Published

March 12, 2025

Introduction

Variceal hemorrhage poses a significant threat to children with chronic liver disease, often leading to severe morbidity and mortality. Octreotide, a somatostatin-analogue, reduces portal venous flow, decreasing blood flow to varices associated with portal hypertension. This retrospective cohort study at Children’s Hospital Los Angeles (CHLA) identified 44 patients who received at least one dose of subcutaneous long-acting octreotide between January 2013 and December 2023. Data on variceal grades, EGD interventions, bleeding episodes, and adverse events were collected.

# 1. Find the "Before" value closest to the treatment date for each patient
before_data <- merged_data %>%
  filter(Date.of.endoscopy < date.of.first.dose) %>%
  group_by(Rand_id) %>%
  filter(Date.of.endoscopy == max(Date.of.endoscopy)) %>%  # Get the closest date before treatment
  select(Rand_id, Grade.of.varices, Date.of.endoscopy, age.at.first.dose, Disease.State)

# 2. Calculate the mean "After" grade for each patient
after_data <- merged_data %>%
  filter(Date.of.endoscopy >= date.of.first.dose) %>%
  group_by(Rand_id) %>%
  summarise(After_Grade = mean(Grade.of.varices, na.rm = TRUE))  # Average after treatment

# 3. Merge before and after data by Rand_id
combined_data <- left_join(before_data, after_data, by = "Rand_id") %>%
  filter(!is.na(After_Grade))  # Ensure both before and after values are present
#### Create a long format for boxplot ####
processed_data_long <- combined_data %>%
  pivot_longer(
    cols = c("Grade.of.varices", "After_Grade"),
    names_to = "TimePeriod",
    values_to = "Grade_of_Varices"
  ) %>%
  mutate(TimePeriod = factor(TimePeriod, levels = c("Grade.of.varices", "After_Grade"),
                             labels = c("Before", "After")))

# Add the 'AgeGroup' column to processed_data_long
processed_data_long$AgeGroup <- cut(processed_data_long$age.at.first.dose,

Conclusion

A Wilcoxon signed-rank test was first used for assessing overall differences in varices grade before and after treatment, yielding a significant reduction (Figure 2, p = 0.014).

We used a linear mixed model with random intercepts for age group to assess the relationship between varices grade and octreotide status, finding a significant reduction in varices grade with treatment (p = 0.00639) while age-related variance was negligible (p > 0.05). Similarly, although autoimmune hepatitis (p = 0.01620) and biliary atresia (p = 0.03888) were associated with higher varices grades, disease state did not significantly alter the effect of octreotide treatment (all p > 0.05), suggesting minimal variability in treatment response due to age or disease state.

The linear mixed models showed significant changes in laboratory values during octreotide therapy. For hemoglobin (before treatment = 9.41), levels increased by 0.22 during therapy (p<0.001) and by 0.57 after therapy (p<0.001). MCV (before treatment = 85.73) decreased 0.83 during therapy (p<0.001) and by 1.67 after therapy (p<0.001). Platelet count (before treatment = 152.67) significantly decreased 45.47 during therapy (p<0.001) and by 16.10 after therapy (p<0.001). Albumin (before treatment = 3.27) increased 0.14 during therapy (p<0.001) and by 0.28 after therapy (p<0.001). Sodium (before treatment = 137.68) decreased 0.88 during therapy (p<0.001) and by 0.46 after therapy (p<0.001). These findings indicate significant, consistent effects of octreotide therapy on these clinical markers.
